\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\textbf{Hardware.}
% \sysname is evaluated on two servers \xiaoxirev{representing a resource-constrained environment and a relatively-abundant resource environment}: \textbf{4$\times$ NVIDIA RTX 4090-24GB} GPUs connected via PCIe 4.0 with an Intel Xeon Gold 6338N CPU (128 cores),
% and \textbf{4$\times$ NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB} GPUs connected via NVLink 3.0 with an Intel Xeon Platinum 8369B CPU (128 cores).
\sysname is evaluated on two servers representing a resource-constrained environment and a relatively resource-abundant environment: \textbf{4$\times$ NVIDIA RTX 4090-24GB} GPUs connected via PCIe 4.0 with an Intel Xeon Gold 6338N CPU (128 cores),
and \textbf{4$\times$ NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB} GPUs connected via NVLink 3.0 with an Intel Xeon Platinum 8369B CPU (128 cores).

\textbf{Models.}
We evaluate \sysname on \textbf{Qwen3-14B}, \textbf{Qwen3-32B}~\citep{qwen3}, and \textbf{Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct}~\citep{llama3} to cover different model scales.

\textbf{Workflows.}
To cover diverse agent execution patterns, we evaluate three representative workflows: \textbf{ReAct}~\citep{ReAct}, \textbf{Plan-and-Solve}~\citep{Plan-and-Solve}, and \textbf{AgentFlow}~\citep{AgentFlow}.

\textbf{Workloads.}
Our evaluation covers four datasets: \textbf{MBPP}~\citep{MBPP}, \textbf{2WikiMultihopQA}~\citep{2wiki}, \textbf{NL2Bash}~\citep{nl2bash}, and \textbf{Spider}~\citep{spider}, corresponding to coding, web search, Bash, and SQL tasks, respectively.

\textbf{Online Traces.}
\label{Traces}
Unlike evaluations based on fixed offline workloads, we use request arrival timestamps from two real-world serving traces, the \textbf{Qwen-Bailian Anonymous Dataset}~\citep{qwen-bailian} and \textbf{Microsoft Azure Traces}~\citep{azure}, to evaluate \sysname under dynamic serving loads. 
The Qwen-Bailian trace exhibits relatively smooth load variation, while the Azure trace shows more pronounced fluctuations. 
Details are provided in~\autoref{online-trace-detail}.

\textbf{Baselines.}
We compare \sysname with \textbf{vLLM Dynamic SD} and \textbf{SGLang Adaptive SD} as adaptive speculative decoding baselines, and \textbf{ThunderAgent} as an agent scheduling baseline.

\textbf{Metrics.}
We use average \textbf{Job Completion Time (JCT)} as the primary metric for end-to-end agent latency, defined as the elapsed time from task arrival to completion of the final workflow step. % 一句话介绍一下附录的P90，P95和吞吐量的实验。
Beyond average JCT, we further report P90/P95 JCT and system throughput in~\autoref{additional_metrics}, showing that \sysname's latency improvements do not come at the cost of tail latency or throughput.

Detailed request characteristics and hardware configurations are provided in~\autoref{request-and-resource-analysis}.

% 合并环境与模型成一段
\textbf{Different Models and Hardware Platforms.}
On the 4090 platform, we deploy Qwen3-14B and Qwen3-32B with TP=2 and TP=4, respectively, to represent resource-constrained settings, characterized by limited KV-cache capacity, lower concurrent-request capacity, lower compute capacity, and low-bandwidth inter-GPU communication.
In contrast, on the A100 platform, we deploy Qwen3-14B, Qwen3-32B, and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct with TP=1, TP=2, and TP=4, respectively, to represent resource-abundant settings, with larger KV-cache capacity, higher concurrent-request capacity, stronger compute capability, and high-speed NVLink interconnects. (See~\autoref{request-and-resource-analysis})

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=10pt]
    \item 
        Under the resource-constrained setting, limited compute capability and memory resources introduce two challenges that \sysname addresses through its SD switching and completion-ware scheduling, respectively.
        Overall, \sysname achieves \textbf{1.31--2.10$\times$} speedup under this setting.
        \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=10pt]
            \item 
                The system becomes compute-bound at relatively small running batch sizes, resulting in a low \xiaoxirev{break-even} point \xiaoxirev{$B^{*}$} beyond which SD no longer provides acceleration.
                Consequently, non-SD \xiaoxirev{(see ThunderAgent in~\autoref{fig:end2end_jct})} is preferred for a large fraction of execution.
                Existing dynamic SD baselines retain additional SD-related overhead in this regime and can even perform worse than pure non-SD. In contrast, \sysname performs a more complete runtime switch and incurs lower overhead in non-SD mode, leading to faster inference and lower average JCT.
            \item 
                Limited KV-cache capacity constrains the runnable batch size and causes more requests to remain queued.
                ThunderAgent primarily optimizes spatial utilization by admitting as many requests as possible within the memory to maximize throughput, whereas \sysname additionally considers the temporal dimension by prioritizing jobs with shorter predicted remaining steps.
                This allows \sysname to complete jobs earlier and further reduce average JCT.
        \end{itemize}
    \item 
        Under the resource-abundant setting, abundant KV-cache capacity substantially reduces queueing, while its stronger compute capability increases the break-even load $B^{*}$ (defined in~\autoref{sec:4.3.1}), allowing SD to remain beneficial under higher serving loads.
        Consequently, there is less room for both scheduling and SD switching optimizations.
        Nevertheless, \sysname still achieves the lowest average JCT, with \textbf{1.18--1.27$\times$} speedup, demonstrating its effectiveness even when the optimization headroom is limited.    
\end{itemize}

% 介绍剩余 Step 预测策略的准确率
\textbf{Remaining-Step Prediction Accuracy.}
Since our scheduler relies on queue ranking, we evaluate the predictor in~\autoref{sec:4.2} using Pair Accuracy and Top-10\% Recall, which measure pairwise ordering accuracy and recall of the true shortest 10\% jobs, respectively.
As shown in~\autoref{tab:prediction_performance}, Type-A achieves 100\% accuracy since its remaining steps are explicitly available from the planning prompt, while Type-B benefits from its multimodal step distribution.
Although Type-C is more difficult to predict, state information still provides useful estimates.
Overall, \sysname achieves \textbf{84.1\% Pair Accuracy} and \textbf{82.2\% Top-10\% Recall}, providing reliable signals for scheduling.

% 介绍消融实验

% 对比调度策略
\textbf{Impact of Scheduling.}
We first evaluate the impact of \sysname's remaining-step-aware scheduling.
Compared with ThunderAgent (\autoref{fig:ablation}, 1), adding the \sysname scheduler (\autoref{fig:ablation}, 4) reduces average JCT from 82.5\,s to 55.1\,s, corresponding to a \textbf{1.50$\times$} speedup.
A similar improvement is observed when combined with vLLM DSD (\autoref{fig:ablation}, 2 vs 5), where average JCT decreases from 72.0\,s to 48.2\,s.
These results show that prioritizing jobs with shorter predicted remaining steps is more effective for reducing average JCT than throughput-oriented scheduling alone.

% 对比不同的动态 SD 实现方式
\textbf{Impact of Dynamic SD Mechanisms.}
We next compare different implementations of dynamic SD.
Replacing vLLM DSD with \sysname's runtime SD switching reduces average JCT from 72.0\,s to 64.6\,s under the ThunderAgent scheduler (\autoref{fig:ablation}, 2 vs 3). 
When combined with the \sysname scheduler, the average JCT further decreases from 48.2\,s with vLLM DSD to 44.5\,s with \sysname (\autoref{fig:ablation}, 5 vs 6).
This improvement mainly comes from \sysname's lower overhead in non-SD mode (see~\autoref{dynamic-sd-comparison}), while preserving comparable performance to the baseline in SD mode.

% 与理想情况的差距
\textbf{Gap to Oracle.}
Finally, we compare \sysname with an oracle configuration that uses oracle remaining-step information for scheduling.
The oracle configuration achieves an average JCT of 43.0\,s, compared with 44.5\,s for \sysname, leaving only a \textbf{3.5\%} gap (\autoref{fig:ablation}, 6 vs 7).
This small gap indicates that the predicted remaining steps are sufficient to achieve performance close to scheduling with ground-truth remaining steps.
